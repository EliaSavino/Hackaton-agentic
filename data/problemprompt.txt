Design a Novel Linker Chemistry with in-silico proof points
Background
Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) are a class of targeted
therapeutics that fuse the precision of monoclonal antibodies
(mAbs) with the high potency of payloads. ADCs offer several
strategic advantages for the drug industry: 1. enable targeted
delivery to diseased tissues to improve the therapeutic index
and reduce off-target toxicity; 2. allow the use of highly potent
payloads that would be too toxic systemically; 3. provide
modular platforms for biomarker-driven patient selection,
supporting precision medicine. An ADC’s performance hinges
on three integrated components: the antibody for tumor
targeting, the payload for bioactivity, and the linker that
connects the payload to the antibody. Although the linker itself
does not exhibit the bioactivity or antigen selectivity, it largely
determines clinical performance of ADC. The linker governs
plasma stability, pharmacokinetics, and the conditional release
of the payload at the tumor site. Cleavable linkers respond to
tumor microenvironment cues (acidic pH, proteases, or
reducing environments) to release payload after internalization.
Non-cleavable linkers rely on antibody degradation, producing
different pharmacology and potentially altered bystander
effects. Payload diversification beyond traditional cytotoxins is
expanding the ADC field’s scope and demanding compatibility
with broader conjugation chemistries and trigger mechanisms.
This diversification aims to broaden therapeutic windows,
overcome resistance, and enable combination strategies.
Problem statement
Many ADCs suffer from suboptimal drug properties (e.g.,
instability, aggregation, poor solubility) and pharmacology
(narrow therapeutic windows, systemic toxicity, and insufficient
tumor payload delivery) largely due to premature payload
release or insufficient release at the tumor site. Existing linkers
often lack tunability to accommodate diverse antibody–payload
combinations and cannot adapt to heterogeneous tumor
environments. There is a critical need for next-generation
linkers that deliver enhanced in vivo stability, precise and
tunable payload release, and broader compatibility with non-
traditional payloads.
Key objectives for linker design include balancing circulation
stability with efficient tumor- or cell-type–specific release while
preserving antibody binding and Fc functions. Goals also
encompass developing tunable cleavage triggers, improving
aqueous solubility, and enabling robust conjugation chemistries
across diverse payload classes (including oligonucleotides and
immunomodulators). Achieving scalable, cost-efficient
manufacturing with consistent product quality will require
integrated linker technologies that enable rapid, reproducible
production and broader therapeutic applicability across
heterogeneous tumor environments.
